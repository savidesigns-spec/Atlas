Apogee — the highest point of an orbit. Apogee Station is a nine-specialist AI crew, commanded by a single orchestrator, built to run HGL's operations at the altitude the name promises: always on, always watching every system at once, never dropping to ground level to babysit any one of them.

Mission Briefing — Why Orbit, Why Now

Agentic AI stopped being a lab demo in 2025. By 2026 every major lab shipped production-grade multi-agent orchestration — the infrastructure to run a coordinated crew, not just a single chat window, finally exists at commercial scale.

Gartner projects over 40% of agentic AI initiatives will be scrapped by 2027 — not because the technology fails, but because most are deployed as one undifferentiated agent asked to do everything, with no command structure and no guardrails. The gap between the 40% that get cancelled and the crews that compound value is architecture, not ambition.

One Astronaut Can't Run a Station

A single generalist agent juggling commerce, security, treasury, content, analytics, market scanning, partnerships, and deal-closing at once is a context bottleneck — it dilutes focus, blends unrelated risk together, and has no internal check on its own mistakes.

Specialized agents orchestrated by a commander outperform monolithic ones on exactly this kind of multi-domain work: each specialist holds a narrow, well-guarded mandate, and the commander composes their output — the same reason a real station runs on a crew, not one astronaut in nine suits.
