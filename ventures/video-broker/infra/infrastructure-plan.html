1 · Read this first

What you won't like.

Five facts that shape every decision in this document.

1. Customer money can pay the vendors, but not on day one.

CERTAIN Stripe pays out a new account's first charges about 7 days after the first payment, up to 14 days in some industries. After that, US payouts run on a 2-business-day rolling schedule. Most AI vendors (fal, BytePlus) are prepaid. That leaves a gap of one to two weeks where customers have paid you but you can't touch the money yet. You need a small starting float, typically $5–10k (§2.3). Or you shrink the gap: charge founder seats only at launch, pay Google on its monthly invoice, and ramp usage with $1 trials first.

2. Prepaid cash isn't yours yet.

CERTAIN When a founder pays for a month, or a customer pays a year up front, you owe them credits. Until those credits are used, the money is a liability (deferred revenue), not profit. Prepaid businesses fail when they spend next month's obligations on this month's salaries. §2.2 has the rules that stop that.

3. Your own servers don't save money at the start.

LIKELY AI model fees will be 80–90%+ of your cost of goods; servers are a rounding error next to them. A self-hosted stack costs roughly €400–600/mo in hardware plus someone who can run it. Managed hosting starts near $0–150/mo with no operations hire. Self-host the heavy, steady pieces (database, video workers, open-weights models) once the numbers justify it, not before (§6–8).

4. Near-zero acquisition spend means slow growth.

GUESSING Organic, referral and creator-credit channels can realistically get you to a few hundred founders in the first 90 days. That's enough to prove demand and fund the vendors. It is not the curve that reaches $250M. That curve needs the capital raise you're planning, spent on paid acquisition once retention data shows it pays back.

5. Higgsfield's look converts. Some of their tactics are a liability for us.

CERTAIN Their live pricing config uses deadline badges ("Buy until: Oct 30"), "70% cheaper" and "BEST VALUE" labels, strike-through prices, "UNLIMITED" badges and auto-refill as the default way to buy. Borrow the visual patterns: the autoplay grid, badges, strike-through, sticky call-to-action, live counters. Skip fake-unlimited offers and fake urgency. Our brand promise is honesty, and the FTC treats fake scarcity as a dark pattern (§9).

2 · Using customer money to pay the vendors

The customer-funded cash loop.

Customers pay first. Credits are issued immediately. Vendor wallets are refilled only from money Stripe has actually paid out. Nothing is spent that hasn't been collected.

2.2 Cash rules (enforce these in code and in banking)

  1. R1: Settled money only. Vendor wallets are refilled only from money Stripe has paid out, never against pending balance or credit lines.
  2. R2: Reserve. Hold 10% of the last 30 days' collections in a separate reserve account for refunds and chargebacks. Stripe may also hold its own reserve on a new AI or subscription account.
  3. R3: Runway alarm. When any vendor wallet drops below 3 days of usage, auto-refill it from operating. If operating can't cover it, the router moves real-time jobs to the Saver queue and pauses new trials. It never overspends.
  4. R4: Unearned cash is locked. Annual prepayments go to an "Unearned" account and move 1/12 per month into operating. The provider cost of credits customers actually use may be drawn early.
  5. R5: Pay postpaid where you can (Google, hosting) and prepaid only where you have to.
  6. R6: Pre-sell founder seats without holding money. Save the customer's card with a Stripe SetupIntent and charge it on launch day. No money is held for a product that doesn't exist yet, which keeps you clear of pre-order holds and refund disputes.

What changes after the capital raise

The cash rules stay. The raise funds three things customer money shouldn't: (1) committed-spend vendor contracts (a 20–50% discount in exchange for prepaying 6–12 months GUESSING), (2) paid acquisition ahead of revenue, and (3) salaries. Customer cash keeps funding the cost of what customers use.

Watch out for

9.2 The loading rules (already built into founders.html)

  1. Poster first. Every tile ships a small AVIF/WebP poster (≤ 40 KB). The video element has preload="none" and no src until needed.
  2. Play only what's visible. An IntersectionObserver attaches the src and plays when a tile is 40% on screen, and pauses when it scrolls away.
  3. At most 3 playing at once. Phones choke on more.
  4. Respect the user. Reduced-motion or data-saver users get posters only.
  5. Short loops. 3–6 seconds, muted, 9:16 at 480×854, H.264 for compatibility plus AV1/VP9 WebM (~30–40% smaller LIKELY). Target 300–600 KB per loop.
  6. HLS only for long videos (over 15 s, or user-generated content in the library): adaptive bitrate through Stream, Mux or Bunny.
  7. Cache forever. Content-hashed filenames, served from the CDN edge with Cache-Control: immutable.
